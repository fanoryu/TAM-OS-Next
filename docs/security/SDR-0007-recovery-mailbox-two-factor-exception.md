> **What this is.** A documented, Owner-approved exception to one SDR-0006 control: the mailbox that can reset the
> Hostinger password may stay without two-factor authentication **for synthetic-data staging only**, under the
> replacement controls in §3.
>
> **What it is not.** It does not state or imply that two-factor authentication is enabled on the Hostinger account or
> on the mailbox. It widens no other part of SDR-0006, authorizes no production deployment, no production cutover and
> no real company data, and does not satisfy SDR-0002 E10. It changes no code, configuration or hosting setting.

---

## 1. Context

SDR-0006 lets synthetic-data staging run on the shared Hostinger account without account two-factor authentication.
Because the account has only one factor, the mailbox that receives its password-reset mail is a second way into it.
SDR-0006 C5 therefore made two-factor authentication on that mailbox mandatory (owner decision D-4 = A), and without
it the exception is not in force.

On 2026-10-26 the Owner decided that:

- Hostinger account two-factor authentication stays **disabled** for synthetic staging, under SDR-0006;
- two-factor authentication will **not** be enabled on the Hostinger recovery mailbox.

## 2. Decision

1. **Mailbox two-factor waived, staging only.** For a deployment covered by SDR-0006, the recovery mailbox need not
   have two-factor authentication. SDR-0006 C5 is read without its two-factor sentence, and the rest of C5 stays: the
   mailbox is company-controlled, not personal, and every person who can read it is on the C1 register and counts
   toward the cap of five.
2. **Replaced, not removed.** The waiver holds only while every mailbox control in §3 is operational and evidenced
   (§4). A lapsed control suspends the SDR-0006 exception exactly as a lapsed SDR-0006 control does.
3. **A weaker state, accepted knowingly.** The Hostinger account and its recovery mailbox are now each protected by a
   single password. The added takeover risk (§5) is accepted by the Owner for synthetic staging only.
4. **Production is unchanged.** Production deployment, production cutover and the first real company data still
   require two-factor authentication on the Hostinger account and a satisfied SDR-0002 E10. This record grants nothing
   for production.
5. **Everything else in SDR-0006 stands:** the synthetic-data restriction (§2.1, §2.6), C1–C4 and C6–C9, X1–X3 and
   X5–X8, residual risks R1–R6, and every revalidation trigger.

## 3. Replacement mailbox controls

| # | Control |
|---|---|
| M1 | **Strong, unique mailbox password.** At least 20 random characters, generated by a password manager, used for no other account, and held only in the C3 shared vault |
| M2 | **Rotated with the Hostinger password.** The mailbox password is rotated on every SDR-0006 C4 occasion: before the first staging deployment under this record, within 24 hours of a reader leaving the register, immediately on any suspected exposure, and at least every 90 days. Each rotation is dated in the register |
| M3 | **Mailbox recovery stays inside the register.** The mailbox's own recovery phone number and recovery email are company-controlled and held only by register members. Personal or unknown recovery options are removed |
| M4 | **No silent diversion.** The mailbox has no auto-forwarding, no forwarding or deletion filter, no delegated access and no connected third-party application beyond those recorded in the register. These are checked and recorded before the first staging deployment and at each 90-day review |
| M5 | **Reset mail is watched.** A register holder checks the mailbox for Hostinger password-reset or security mail before each staging session and at least every working day while a staging deployment exists. Any unexplained reset or security mail is an SDR-0006 §6 revalidation trigger |
| M6 | **Mailbox sign-in monitoring where available.** Any login alert, sign-in history or "other sessions" view the mail provider offers is enabled and reviewed with C8; open sessions not belonging to register holders are signed out. If the provider offers none, that absence is recorded as a gap, not as met |
| M7 | **Where the mailbox is hosted is recorded.** If the mailbox is hosted inside the same Hostinger account, that is recorded, because one compromised Hostinger login then also controls the recovery path (§5, R9) |

C9 (trusted devices only) applies to mailbox access exactly as to hPanel access.

## 4. Evidence

Evidence row **X4** of SDR-0006 §4 is read as:

| # | Evidence | State at acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| X4 | C5 recovery mailbox identified, company-controlled, readers on the register; its two-factor state recorded as found (disabled, per the Owner, 2026-10-26); M1–M7 evidenced | Not yet evidenced |

Facts and dates only are recorded, in the private layer — never a password, recovery code or key. Until X4 in this
form and every other SDR-0006 X-item are evidenced, the SDR-0006 exception is not in force and no staging deployment
relies on it.

## 5. Residual risk (accepted by the Owner for synthetic staging only)

This record replaces the SDR-0006 R7 statement and adds two risks:

| # | Risk | Why the controls do not remove it |
|---|---|---|
| R7 | **Recovery-path takeover.** Whoever controls the recovery mailbox can reset the Hostinger password and take the account | M1–M6 reduce the likelihood and speed up detection; with one factor on the mailbox, a correct stolen mailbox password still opens the path |
| R8 | **Two single-factor doors.** An attacker can take the account through either the Hostinger password or the mailbox password; a phishing kit, infostealer or reused-password leak against either one is enough | M1, M2, C2–C4 keep both strong and rotated; neither has a second factor |
| R9 | **Circular recovery** (if M7 records the mailbox inside the same Hostinger account). One compromised Hostinger login also controls the recovery mailbox, so the attacker can lock out the holders and reset at will | Recorded only; removing it would need the mailbox moved elsewhere or two-factor enabled |

**Wider than staging.** A takeover through either door reaches the whole Hostinger account, not just TAM OS staging:
the production database `u360995908_tamos_prod`, the company's `reliabilityindonesia.com` web presence, DNS-adjacent
settings and provider backups. As SDR-0006 §5 states, the risk to those assets is **not** accepted or assessed by
SDR-0006 or by this record; it is an existing exposure of the shared account that the Owner carries. Before real data
reaches the production database, SDR-0002 still requires Hostinger account two-factor authentication.

## 6. Revalidation triggers

Every SDR-0006 §6 trigger applies. In addition, re-examine this record, and treat the SDR-0006 exception as suspended
until the Owner re-confirms it, if:

- any M-control lapses or its evidence is no longer true;
- the mailbox provider, the mailbox's hosting location or its recovery options change;
- unexplained mail-forwarding rules, filters, delegates or sessions are found on the mailbox.

This record **ends** when two-factor authentication is enabled on the recovery mailbox (SDR-0006 C5 then applies as
written) or when the SDR-0006 exception ends.
